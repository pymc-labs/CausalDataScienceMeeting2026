01The problem

Sales show what happened.
Not what caused it.

Different specifications can explain similar sales histories. They can give different contributions to the same channel.

An accurate sales forecast does not necessarily tell you what each channel caused.

Step 0: weekly sales. One line. This is all that the data show.

Steps 1 to 3: three decompositions. The total stays the same in every one. Only the split changes.

Key point: predicting sales does not determine contributions. The paper names two problems: structural uncertainty and repeated inference. This slide shows the first.

01The problem

A marketing mix model is a structural model

Intrinsic baseline. The part of sales that no input explains. It can change over time.
Channel contributions. Media spend after carryover (adstock) and a response curve, saturating or linear.
Control contributions. Price, holidays and other measured factors that are not media.

Source: Eq. (1) in Section 2.2 of the paper. Graphs are illustrative. Arrow: assumed direct effect. Dashed line: observed dependency. Dashed circle: hidden variable.

The model writes sales as a sum. Baseline, each channel, each control, plus noise.

Each term is a latent quantity. We never observe it. We observe only the sum.

Step 4: the same equation is a causal graph. Every arrow is an assumption: this input acts on sales directly.

Step 5: three graphs that fit the same weekly data. Dashed lines are what the data show. Arrows are what we assume.

One: TV and search move together. The data show the correlation, not which arrow carries the effect. River's prior contains this case.

Two: hidden demand drives both search spending and sales. Part of the "Search effect" is demand. River's prior does not contain hidden demand yet.

Three: search spending follows the promotion calendar. That is a path between two inputs. River's prior has no paths between inputs. Part 5 comes back to both.

The usual order: assume one thing, fit, check, change, fit again. It is sequential.

We reverse it. We assume many things at the start, in a prior. We learn inference once. Then each new dataset needs only sampling with fixed weights, not a new fit.

A shorter loop is a goal. Backup B2 and Section 4.5 of the paper give the measured cost and its caveats.

The Goal

Not a number.
A distribution over graphs and contributions.

  1. 1A set of graphs of direct effects that can explain the data.
  2. 2For each graph, a distribution of contributions over time.

The outcome is not one number: it is a distribution over graphs and contributions.

River returns graphs and, for each graph, contributions. Now we look at the simulated worlds used to learn this distribution.

02Simulated causal worlds

Use the prior to generate worlds with a known truth

Each world has its own inputs, causal links, response curves and sales. The simulator stores the graph and every contribution.

200,000 worlds208 weeks 1–10 channels1–10 controls50 prior combinations

In the middle 90% of worlds, channels give from 5.1% to 70.9% of sales.

Inputs move together. In the median world, the strongest correlation between two inputs is 0.94.

Similar data can come from different causal worlds. River learns a distribution of explanations, not one answer.

Source: Sections 2.1 and 2.3, Table 1 and Appendix A.2 of the paper. 196,000 training and 4,000 holdout worlds; 50 equally weighted combinations. Correlation: median of 1,000 sampled training worlds.

We write the plausible specifications into one prior. We use the prior to generate worlds with a known truth.

Step 1: one world. It has a graph of direct effects and a decomposition. Both are known, because we generated them.

Step 2: the worlds are wide and hard on purpose. Media can give a small or a large part of sales. Inputs move together, a known problem in marketing mix models. So similar data can come from different worlds, and River must return a distribution of explanations.

02The causal target

A contribution is an interventional quantity

In River’s graph, the contribution of a channel is the change in noise-free sales when its full spend path is set to zero and every other draw stays fixed.

The boundary of River’s prior

Only input → sales edges. No paths between inputs. No hidden demand. River learns which permitted edges are present.

Source: Section 2.2, Appendix A.1.1 and Eq. (3) of the paper.

For a causal audience, this is the important definition. A contribution is not a regression coefficient. It is the effect of an intervention on the whole spend path.

Step 1: we set TV spend to zero for all weeks. Noise-free sales drop. The gap is the TV contribution.

Step 2: the boundary. The graph family is restricted: direct effects only, no confounding by hidden demand, no input-to-input paths. River learns which permitted edges are present.

02Amortized inference · prior-fitted networks

Learn the posterior from the prior

Simulate worlds with a known truth. Train one network to recover that truth from the data an analyst sees.

ideal loss

If the network is flexible enough, the best answer is the model-averaged posterior.

River’s flow-matching loss aims at the same target, without a guarantee of exact draws.

optimum

Source: Eqs. (19), (21) and (22) in Appendix B.1, and Appendix B.9 of the paper; prior-fitted networks (Müller et al., 2022).

Step 0: the idea. Draw worlds from the prior and store the graph and the contributions. Train one network on what an analyst would see, with the stored truth as targets. In symbols, the ideal loss is the negative log density of the truth. River does not use this exact loss; step 1 explains.

Step 1: the main message. The ideal loss equals an expected KL divergence to the true posterior, plus a constant. So, if the network can represent it, the optimum is the posterior of the mixture prior: Bayesian model averaging over every specification in the prior, learned once. River minimizes the squared error of a flow velocity, plus an auxiliary edge loss. That loss is not a KL divergence, but the paper treats the same posterior as its intended target.

The limits: the ideal needs data from the prior, a flexible enough network, exact minimization and exact integration. River trains on a fixed set of 196,000 worlds, each at six context lengths, for about 3.4 passes over these views, and samples with 16 Euler steps. So the posterior is the intended target, not a guarantee. A new dataset is read with fixed weights; outside the prior, there is no such result.

03Inside River

One dataset in

Spend and controls for every week. Sales only for the context weeks. Query sales are zero-filled and flagged. The true graph and contributions never enter the encoder.

A shared transformer encoder

Attention runs across weeks and across inputs. Time attention is causal: a cell cannot attend to later weeks directly. Four blocks, width 128, eight heads.

The joint router

A flow head that generates a graph and a decomposition together. Its target appends the graph, coded −1 / +1, to the contributions: 346 coordinates per week.

A sampled graph G

An edge is present when its mean signed state over the query weeks is above zero. River keeps the graph and discards the router’s decomposition.

The graph conditioner

One round of messages along the sampled edges between inputs, latent demand, baseline and sales. 321 graph slots in 8 families; River’s prior uses only the 20 direct ones.

The conditional decoder

A second flow head generates contributions for that graph. A support mask removes forbidden coordinates: an absent edge gives exactly zero.

One decomposition draw

Baseline, each channel and each control, for every week of the requested window. 25 coordinates per week, including four latent and indirect slots that River’s prior sets to zero.

Training

The simulator’s graph and contributions are the targets. The decoder learns with the true graph. An auxiliary edge classifier adds a training loss; checkpoint selection only checks that its held-out score is valid. It never selects graphs.

Inference: weights fixed

In the evaluation, each of 256 draws samples a graph with the router, then decodes contributions for that graph. Each flow head takes 16 Euler steps per draw.

We assemble the network piece by piece. The camera moves to each part in turn.

0: the input is 208 weeks by 21 rows: up to 10 channels, 10 controls and sales, with 31 features per cell. Context and query windows.

1: the encoder. Time attention is lower-triangular. Entity attention is bidirectional over all cells of a week, inputs and sales.

2 and 3: the router generates contributions and a graph together. We keep only the graph.

4 and 5: the conditioner adjusts the representations for that graph. The decoder draws contributions for it. Absent edges give exact zeros.

6: one draw of the decomposition: baseline, channels and controls for every week of the window.

7: training uses the true graph for the decoder, and the simulated contributions as targets. Loss: joint and conditional velocity losses for query and context windows, plus an edge classification loss. The evaluated checkpoint is the last one of a fixed 62,500-update budget.

8: inference repeats graph, then contributions. The evaluation used 256 draws; the structure study used 2,048 router draws.

03Inside River · The causal core

At inference, River samples the causal graph

The prior permits one edge from each input to sales. The true graph is not given. River infers which edges are present.

Each draw starts with a graph from the router.

Over many draws, the graphs give a probability for each edge. In the evaluation, the calibration error of the channel-edge probabilities is 0.04 to 0.07.

For each graph, the decoder draws contributions. An input without an edge contributes exactly zero, so each contribution posterior mixes a point mass at zero with a continuous part.

Together, they form the learned posterior: variation between graphs and within each graph.

Source: Sections 3, 4.3 and B.6.1, Eq. (32) of the paper.

This is the causal core of River. It is conditional discovery: structure inferred from the data, conditional on the prior's graph family.

Step 1: 256 graph draws, the setting of the contribution evaluation; the structure study used 2,048 router draws per view. Step 2: their frequencies are edge probabilities.

Step 3: absent edges give exact zeros. A channel's contribution posterior is a spike at zero plus a slab. Graph uncertainty enters the contribution draws.

Step 4: Eq. (32) is the network's counterpart of the model-averaged posterior in Eq. (19). Graphs take the place of specifications. Response functions, carryover choices and input settings enter through the training mixture of 50 combinations.

04Evidence · How we test

One world. Five analysts.

A simulated world. The simulator knows the true graph and every contribution. An analyst sees only the inputs and the sales of the context weeks.

Five conditions, 100 cases each. Three conditions select the worlds. Two conditions hide the largest-effect channel or control, outside River’s training prior.

Five analysts get the same table. No analyst sees the true contributions. Only the oracle also gets the true graph and the mechanisms. It runs on the full-input cases only.

Scores against the held-out truth. CRPS compares each predicted distribution with the true value. Lower is better. 500 cases at three horizons give 1,500 scored views.

Source: Section 4.1 of the paper. 300 distinct held-out worlds; the full-input and both omission conditions share 100 base worlds.

Step 0, the world. It holds the observations, the held-out truth, and the true graph and mechanisms. Analysts get the input histories and the context sales. They do not get the true decomposition or the query sales.

Step 1, the conditions: 100 cases each. The first three select the worlds. The last two hide the largest-effect channel or control, so they are out of prior. They are not five transformations of every world: the 500 cases come from 300 distinct worlds.

Step 2, the analysts. Every method gets the same table. The oracle is a fitted reference with privileged information: the generating graph, the mechanism families and prior information. No analyst has it, and it is not a guaranteed best case. It runs on the 100 full-input cases only. PyMC-Marketing runs in all five conditions. A separate PyMC-Marketing study also uses the 100 full-input worlds.

Step 3, the scores. We compare each method's contributions and sales predictions with the held-out truth. CRPS scores a whole predicted distribution against the true value. Three horizons: 99 context weeks with 109 query weeks, 185 with 23, and 207 with 1. That gives 1,500 scored views. The structure study on the next slide scores graph recovery.

04Evidence · Structure

River finds most direct effects without the true graph

Source: Section 4.3, Figure 3 and Appendix C.10 of the paper. Structure study of run pr29-final-full-e35866e: router, 100 full-input worlds, 2,048 graph draws per view.

Say: River does not get the true graph. We score its sampled graphs on the 100 full-input worlds. Edge by edge, it is right about nine times in ten. The whole set is right less often: the exact channels in about half of the worlds, channels and controls together in fewer than one in five. This is partial structure recovery, under the specified prior.

Edge by edge: precision and recall are 0.89 and 0.90 with 99 context weeks, 0.89 and 0.91 with 185, and 0.92 and 0.87 with 207. An edge counts when its marginal probability is at least 0.5. In one draw, an edge is present when its mean signed state over the query weeks is above zero. Its probability is the share of 2,048 draws that contain it. There is no recalibration and no threshold tuning.

Whole set: the exact channel set in 45%, 51% and 49% of worlds; channels and controls together in 17%, 19% and 19%.

If asked about the draws: the true complete graph is among the 2,048 draws in 82, 84 and 86 of 100 worlds at 99, 185 and 207 context weeks. Full-graph calibration is not established.

If asked about calibration: the edge-pooled calibration error (ECE) for channel edges is 0.040, 0.039 and 0.067. These are measurements, not proof of calibration. At 99 and 207 context weeks, the lowest bin is too low: edges with a probability of about 0.03 are present in about 11% of cases; at 185 weeks, in about 7%. At 207 context weeks, edges rated 0.3 to 0.8 are present more often than their rating.

04Evidence · Contributions

Lower contribution error in every condition

Source: Section 4.2 and Tables 12 and 20 of the paper. CRPS of window-mean contributions on the context sales scale. Lower is better.

Say: CRPS scores a whole distribution against the true value. Each row is one condition at one context length. River has the lowest mean error of the four methods in all 15 rows. This is also true when we hide the largest-effect channel or control.

Full inputs, 99 context weeks: 0.0295 for River, 0.0370 for PathMC, 0.2136 for OLS and 0.0659 for PyMC-Marketing.

A lower mean is not a win in every world. River beats both regressions on the same world in a majority of worlds in 11 of 15 rows. Against PathMC, only the omitted-channel row at 99 context weeks has a paired interval that includes zero.

PyMC-Marketing is the native MMM of Table 12: version 1.2, a time-varying intercept, eight-lag geometric adstock, logistic saturation and library-default priors.

If asked, the separate PyMC-Marketing study (Table 20, the 100 full-input worlds): the MMM/River ratio of mean channel CRPS is 2.11 at 99 context weeks, 2.44 at 185 and 2.20 at 207. These are ratios of cohort means, not typical world-level ratios. Each fit uses eight chains of 500 draws. 288 of the 300 MMM fits fail at least one health criterion, and none is excluded. The scores describe this configured procedure, not a converged MMM.

05Boundaries · Limitations

Limitations of River

  1. 01Direct effects onlyThe prior has no confounding from hidden demand and no paths between inputs. If the data contain them, the results can be unreliable.
  2. 02Reliable inside the prior; outside it, only on some axesWith 12 channels (training had at most 10), contributions stay the most accurate. With the largest input hidden, the visible inputs stay accurate, but River does not find the hidden cause. With a different input dependence, intervals cover less. Other axes are not tested.
  3. 03Contribution intervals miss the truth too often80% intervals contain the true window mean in 59% of cases for channels and 68% for controls (full inputs, 99 context weeks). The channel intervals of PathMC, OLS and the MMM miss even more often.
  4. 04No check for approximation errorRiver has no chains, so divergence and mixing checks do not apply. Approximation error remains, and no check on a single dataset measures it.

Source: Sections 2, 4.2, 4.4, 4.6, 4.7, 4.8 and 5 of the paper; the 80% coverage table and the weekly coverage figure in Appendix C.

Say: a limitation here is a property of River that restricts where you can trust its output. Two things are not on this list on purpose. The oracle does better because it gets the true graph. River is not built to forecast sales.

01: the prior permits only direct effects from each input to sales. Inference can be unreliable when the prior does not describe the data. Slide 18 lists recovery under hidden confounding as not established.

02: twelve-channel cohort: channel CRPS 0.0140, 0.0129 and 0.0135, below PathMC, OLS and the native MMM. This supports the tested larger population, not any number of channels. Largest input hidden (out of prior): lower mean contribution scores on the visible inputs; the hidden input is not recovered, and in one example the baseline error rises. Real-data-informed settings: no detected increase in channel point error; 90% coverage of non-zero effects is 71.0% for K7 and 54.9% for M4, against 73.0% for the corpus reference. Not tested: confounding, paths between inputs, unseen response shapes and real business data.

03: exact values 58.9% for channels and 68.0% for controls. The intervals under-cover in every benchmark condition. Bias, not only width, can cause this. Weekly 95% coverage of live channels, read from the figure: River about 85–92%, PathMC about 57–61%, OLS about 42–54%, native MMM about 78–80%, oracle about 90–93%. River's channel intervals are also narrower than those of OLS and the MMM: 12–14% of the sales scale, against 35–90% and 46–62%.

04: River's draws avoid MCMC divergences and chain-mixing failures, but not approximation error. The paper measures calibration over cohorts of worlds, not for one dataset.

If asked about the oracle: it gets the true graph, the mechanism families and prior information, so a gap is expected. It shows headroom, not a limit. Mean channel CRPS is 0.0185, 0.0141 and 0.0139, against 0.0295, 0.0241 and 0.0263 for River. The oracle is a fitted reference, so its score is not a lower bound.

If asked about sales prediction: River is built for the decomposition, not as a forecaster. For the one-week query, mean total-sales CRPS is 0.0384 for River, 0.0213 for PathMC, 0.0290 for OLS and 0.0085 for the native MMM. With 23 query weeks: 0.0422 against 0.0312, 0.0388 and 0.0206. With 109 query weeks, River has the lowest mean in all five conditions.

If asked about the sales bands: one-week 95% bands hold sales in 99 of 100 worlds, but they are 49.5% of the sales scale wide, against 12.1% for the oracle. River has no observation-noise draws. The paper does not test why the bands are wide; averaging over graphs is a hypothesis.

05Conclusions · What is demonstrated

What River demonstrates
in the tested synthetic setting

  1. 01Contribution inferenceLower mean channel CRPS than PathMC, OLS and PyMC-Marketing in all five conditions and at all three horizons. In a separate study, less than half the channel CRPS of a default PyMC-Marketing model; most of its fits fail a health check.
  2. 02Constrained structure recoveryChannel-edge precision and recall near 0.9, with a calibration error of 0.04 to 0.07 for the edge probabilities. The exact channel set in about half of the worlds; the sampled graphs contain the true graph in most worlds.
  3. 03Reusable inferenceOne trained network serves new datasets with no new fit. Tested positive changes of units do not change the answer.

Graph draws describe structural uncertainty under the prior’s assumptions. They do not establish causal identification from business data.

Source: Sections 4, 5 and 6 of the paper.

Say: three results, each tested against known truth on held-out synthetic worlds, with no new fit. River is a first tool, not the finished tool.

If asked what is not established: full DAG discovery, learned edge directions, recovery under hidden confounding, transfer to response mechanisms outside the prior, and causal identification from real business data. The logistic condition uses a response family seen in training. The omission tests do not show recovery of the missing cause.

If asked about the source of the gain: the comparison evaluates complete methods. It does not isolate graph sampling as the cause of the better contribution scores.

05Future work · The next stage

The graph slots are already in the network

Today each graph that River samples holds for every week: direct effects only.

The network keeps 321 graph slots in 8 families. River’s prior uses the 20 direct ones and sets the other 301, for hidden demand and paths between inputs, to zero. They are capacity, not learned effects.

The next research stage: mediation, interactions, and recovery under hidden demand. New priors, new weights, new tests.

And a structure that can change over time: time-varying causal graphs.

Source: Appendix B.5 and Sections 5 and 5.2 of the paper. Time-varying graphs: our research direction, not part of the paper.

The architecture is ahead of the prior. Slots for latent demand and input-to-input edges exist, with zero training targets.

Mediation: an input acts through another variable. Awareness: marketing builds a state that affects later sales. Funnel: stages from awareness to purchase. Interactions: one input changes another's effect.

Time-varying graphs are our own research direction. The paper does not cover them.

B1Backup · Inside River · How a head draws

Flow matching turns noise into draws

A flow head starts from random noise. In 16 small steps it moves the noise to one draw. Each step reads the encoded dataset h: the noise selects the draw, the data control the distribution of the draws.

Training picks a point on the straight line from noise to the true values of a world. The network sees that point and the encoded data, not the truth. It learns the expected velocity toward the truth.

h: encoded dataset (for the decoder, after the graph conditioner). κ: shared noise of the draw. W: mask weights.

At inference, the head follows its learned velocities from fresh noise. Sixteen Euler steps. Different noise gives different draws.

The base noise has a shared part over weeks. So each draw starts as one coherent path, not as independent weekly values.

Source: Section 3 and Appendix B.6 (Eqs. (28)–(30)) of the paper; conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Dax et al., 2023). Checked against pymc-mfm 32587ac (training) and 44521b6 (inference in the paper's evaluation).

Both heads are flow-matching heads. Here is how one head makes a draw. The router reads the encoded dataset and draws a graph and contributions together; we keep only the graph. The decoder reads the encoded dataset after the graph conditioner, and the support mask of that graph. It draws the contributions for that graph.

Step 1: training. Straight lines from noise to truth. The loss is the squared error of the velocity, not a point-estimate loss on the contributions. W gives zero weight to padded or forbidden coordinates. For the router, the decomposition and each graph family get equal weight per world.

Step 2: inference. Start from new noise. Follow the field for 16 Euler steps. The picture uses the exact velocity field of a target with two modes, one for each graph. The router meets such targets; the decoder sees one graph at a time. River's network learns only an approximation of this field.

Step 3: the noise has a shared time component. So each draw starts as a coherent path over weeks. The head computes the week pattern of this component from the encoded data: four week patterns, with random weights for each coordinate and draw.
