01The problem

Sales show what happened.
Not what caused it.

Marketing teams plan TV, search and promotions as one system. The inputs move together, so different splits of sales fit the same history.

A good sales forecast does not tell you what each channel caused.

Step 0: weekly sales. One line. This is all that the data show.

Steps 1 to 3: three decompositions. The total stays the same in every one. Only the split changes.

Key point: predicting sales does not determine contributions. The central problem is contribution inference under structural uncertainty.

01The problem

A marketing mix model is a structural model

Intrinsic baseline. The part of sales that no input explains. It can change over time.
Channel contributions. Media spend after carryover (adstock) and a response curve, saturating or linear.
Control contributions. Price, holidays and other measured factors that are not media.

Eq. (3) of the paper. Graphs are illustrative. Arrow: assumed direct effect. Dashed line: observed dependency. Dashed circle: hidden variable.

The model writes sales as a sum. Baseline, each channel, each control, plus noise.

Each term is a latent quantity. We never observe it. We observe only the sum.

Step 4: the same equation is a causal graph. Every arrow is an assumption: this input acts on sales directly.

Step 5: three graphs that fit the same weekly data. Dashed lines are what the data show. Arrows are what we assume.

One: TV and search move together. The data show the correlation, not which arrow carries the effect. River's prior contains this case.

Two: hidden demand drives both the budget and sales. Part of the "TV effect" is demand. River's prior does not contain hidden demand yet.

Three: the TV plan follows the promotion calendar. That is a path between two inputs. River's prior has no paths between inputs. Part 5 comes back to both.

Every analysis makes four kinds of choices. One of them is the causal structure: the three graphs from the previous slide.

Steps 1 and 2: two analysts, two sets of choices, two different channel splits. Both fit the data equally well.

Step 3: selecting one model conditions on that choice. The intervals then ignore the other models.

Step 4: the principled answer is model averaging. But each specification needs its own MCMC fit, and nothing checks the choices against the truth.

The usual order: assume one thing, fit, check, change, fit again. It is sequential.

We reverse it. We assume many things at the start, in a prior. We learn inference once. Then each dataset is a forward pass.

A shorter loop is a goal. Part 4 gives the measured cost and its caveats.

02Simulated causal worlds

Use the prior to generate worlds with a known truth

Each world has its own inputs, causal links, response curves and sales. The simulator stores the graph and every contribution.

200,000 worlds208 weeks 1–10 channels1–10 controls50 prior combinations

In the middle 90% of worlds, channels give from 5.1% to 70.9% of sales.

Inputs move together. In the median world, the strongest correlation between two inputs is 0.94.

Similar data can come from different causal worlds. River learns a distribution of explanations, not one answer.

Section 2 and Appendix A of the paper. 196,000 training and 4,000 holdout worlds; 50 equally weighted combinations. Correlation: median of 1,000 sampled training worlds.

We write the plausible specifications into one prior. We use the prior to generate worlds with a known truth.

Step 1: one world. It has a graph of direct effects and a decomposition. Both are known, because we generated them.

Step 2: the worlds are wide and hard on purpose. Media can give a small or a large part of sales. Inputs move together, a known problem in marketing mix models. So similar data can come from different worlds, and River must return a distribution of explanations.

02The causal target

A contribution is an interventional quantity

In River’s graph, the contribution of a channel is the change in noise-free sales when its full spend path is set to zero and every other draw stays fixed.

The boundary of River’s prior

Only input → sales edges. No paths between inputs. No hidden demand. River learns which permitted edges are present.

Section 2.2 and Appendix A.1.1 of the paper.

For a causal audience, this is the important definition. A contribution is not a regression coefficient. It is the effect of an intervention on the whole spend path.

Step 1: we set TV spend to zero for all weeks. Noise-free sales drop. The gap is the TV contribution.

Step 2: the boundary. The graph family is restricted: direct effects only, no confounding by hidden demand, no input-to-input paths. River learns which permitted edges are present.

02Amortized inference · prior-fitted networks

Learn the posterior from the prior

train
equals
optimum

The result

If the network is flexible enough, the loss is smallest at the model-averaged posterior of the mixture prior.

1 · Simulate

Draw worlds from the prior. Store the graph and the contributions.

2 · Train

Teach one network to return the stored truth from what an analyst would see.

3 · Use

Read a new dataset with fixed weights. Outside the prior, no guarantee holds.

Eqs. (1), (2) and Appendix B.1 of the paper; prior-fitted networks (Müller et al., 2022).

This is the prior-fitted network argument. Train on pairs of datasets and contributions from the prior. Minimize the negative log density of the truth.

Step 1: the loss is an expected KL divergence to the true posterior, plus a constant.

Step 2: so the optimum is the posterior of the mixture prior. That is Bayesian model averaging over every specification in the prior, learned once.

Step 3: the honest part. Finite data and optimization do not guarantee the ideal. River uses flow matching, which has the same intended target. Outside the prior, there is no such result.

The output

Not a number.
A distribution over graphs and contributions.

  1. 1A set of graphs of direct effects that can explain the data.
  2. 2For each graph, a posterior of contributions over time.

The outcome is not one number: it is a distribution over graphs and contributions.

River returns graphs and, for each graph, contributions. Now we open the network.

03Inside River

One dataset in

Spend and controls for every week. Sales only for the context weeks. Query sales are zero-filled and flagged. The true graph and contributions never enter the encoder.

A shared transformer encoder

Attention runs across weeks and across inputs. Time attention is causal: a cell cannot attend to later weeks directly. Four blocks, width 128, eight heads.

The joint router

A flow head that generates a graph and a decomposition together. Its target appends the graph, coded −1 / +1, to the contributions: 346 coordinates per week.

A sampled graph G

An edge is present when its mean signed state over the query weeks is above zero. The router’s own decomposition is discarded.

The graph conditioner

One round of messages along the sampled edges between inputs, latent demand, baseline and sales. 321 graph slots in 8 families; River’s prior uses only the 20 direct ones.

The conditional decoder

A second flow head generates contributions for that graph. A support mask removes forbidden coordinates: an absent edge gives exactly zero.

One decomposition draw

Baseline, each channel and each control, for every week of the requested window. 25 coordinates per week, including four latent and indirect slots that River’s prior sets to zero.

Training

The simulator’s graph and contributions are the targets. The decoder learns with the true graph. An auxiliary edge classifier serves training and checkpoint selection only; it never selects graphs.

Inference: weights fixed

For each of 256 draws: sample a graph with the router, then decode contributions for that graph. Each flow head takes 16 Euler steps per draw.

We assemble the network piece by piece. The camera zooms into each part.

0: the input tensor. 208 weeks by 21 rows: up to 10 channels, 10 controls and sales. 31 features per cell. Context and query windows.

1: the encoder. Time attention is lower-triangular. Entity attention is bidirectional over the inputs of a week.

2 and 3: the router generates contributions and a graph together. We keep only the graph.

4 and 5: the conditioner adjusts the representations for that graph. The decoder draws contributions for it. Absent edges give exact zeros.

7: training uses the true graph for the decoder, and the simulated contributions as targets. Loss: joint and conditional velocity losses for query and context windows, plus an edge classification loss.

8: inference repeats graph, then contributions, 256 times.

03Inside River · How a head draws

Flow matching turns noise into draws

A flow head starts from random noise and moves it, in small steps, toward a plausible value of the contributions.

Training picks a point on the straight line from noise to the true values of a world. The network learns the velocity toward the truth.

At inference, the head follows its learned velocities from fresh noise. Sixteen Euler steps. Different noise gives different draws.

The noise is coherent over time, so weeks within one draw move together.

Section 3.3 and Appendix B.6 of the paper; conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Dax et al., 2023).

Both heads are flow-matching heads. Here is how one head makes a draw.

Step 1: training. Straight lines from noise to truth. The loss is the squared error of the velocity, not a point-estimate loss on the contributions.

Step 2: inference. Start from new noise. Follow the field for 16 steps. The target here has two modes, one for each graph. The flow finds both.

Step 3: the noise has a shared time component, so a draw is a coherent path over weeks, not independent weekly values.

03Inside River · The causal core

At inference, River samples the causal graph

The prior permits one edge from each input to sales. The true graph is not given. River infers which edges are present.

Each draw starts with a graph from the router.

Over many draws, the graphs give a probability for each edge. In the evaluation, channel-edge probabilities are close to calibrated at the margin.

For each graph, the decoder draws contributions. An input without an edge contributes exactly zero, so each contribution posterior mixes a point mass at zero with a continuous part.

Together, they form the learned posterior: variation between graphs and within each graph.

Section 3.4, Eq. (4) of the paper.

This is the causal core of River. It is conditional discovery: structure inferred from the data, conditional on the prior's graph family.

Step 1: 256 graph draws. Step 2: their frequencies are edge probabilities.

Step 3: absent edges give exact zeros. A channel's contribution posterior is a spike at zero plus a slab. Graph uncertainty enters the contribution draws.

Step 4: Eq. (4) is the network's counterpart of model averaging, with graphs in place of specifications. Response functions and input settings enter through the training mixture.

04Evidence · Structure

River finds most direct effects from the data alone

The true graph is not supplied at inference. We score the sampled graphs on the 100 full-input worlds. Two questions stay separate.

Edge by edge. An edge counts when its marginal probability is at least 0.5. Channel edges reach a precision and recall near 0.9.

Calibration at the margin. Points near the diagonal mean that a stated edge probability matches how often the edge is present. Edge-pooled calibration error for channel edges: 0.04 to 0.07.

Whole set. The exact set of live channels is right in about half of the worlds. The full graph, with controls, in fewer than one in five.

Partial structure recovery, under the specified prior.

Section 4.3 and Appendix C.9 of the paper; reliability bins from the structure-accuracy run (router, channel edges).

The headline causal result. Good edge-level scores do not mean every graph is right. We report both.

Step 1: precision and recall 0.89 and 0.90 with 99 context weeks; 0.92 and 0.87 with 207.

Step 2: reliability diagram from the actual bins of the structure run. Channel edges only. The lowest bin is under-confident: edges rated about 0.03 are present about 11% of the time.

Step 3: exact channel set in 45% and 49% of worlds; channels and controls together in 17% and 19%. Full-graph calibration is not established.

05Boundaries · It says where it fails

Where River falls short

  1. 01The oracle still does betterTold the true graph and mechanisms, the oracle has lower channel CRPS: 0.0185 against River’s 0.0294 with 99 context weeks.
  2. 02Contributions improve; one-week sales do notFor the one-week query, total-sales CRPS is 0.0384 for River, 0.0188 for PathMC and 0.0290 for OLS.
  3. 03Uncertainty is not yet calibratedOne-week 95% bands hold sales in 99 of 100 worlds, but they are 49.5% of the sales scale wide, against 12.1% for the oracle. Long-query contribution intervals are too narrow.
  4. 04The gain is not yet attributedThe comparison evaluates complete methods. It does not isolate graph sampling as the cause of the better contribution scores.

Sections 4.2, 4.5, 4.6 and 5.1 of the paper.

The advantage is in the decomposition. It is not general prediction superiority.

Wide bands that cover the truth are not evidence of a better predictive distribution.

05Boundaries · The causal claim

What is demonstrated, and what is not

Demonstrated now

Inference in a restricted direct-effect setting

Contribution inference and partial recovery of permitted direct effects, on held-out synthetic worlds, with no new fit.

Not yet established

Unrestricted causal discovery

Full DAG discovery, learned edge directions, recovery under hidden confounding, new response mechanisms, and identification from real business data.

Research goal

Richer causal structure

Mediation, awareness, funnel effects and interactions. Each needs a broader prior, new weights and new evaluation.

Graph draws describe structural uncertainty under the prior’s assumptions. They do not establish causal identification from business data.

Sections 5.1 and 5.3 of the paper.

We keep three statements separate: what is demonstrated, what is not, and the research goal.

The logistic condition uses a response family seen in training. The omission tests do not show recovery of the missing cause.

05The next stage

The graph slots are already in the network

Today each graph that River samples holds for every week: direct effects only.

The network keeps 321 graph slots in 8 families. River’s prior uses the 20 direct ones and sets the other 301, for hidden demand and paths between inputs, to zero. They are capacity, not learned effects.

The next research stage: mediation, interactions, and recovery under hidden demand. New priors, new weights, new tests.

And a structure that can change over time: time-varying causal graphs.

Appendix B.5 and Sections 5.1 and 5.3 of the paper. Time-varying graphs: our research direction, not part of the paper.

The architecture is ahead of the prior. Slots for latent demand and input-to-input edges exist, with zero training targets.

Mediation: an input acts through another variable. Awareness: marketing builds a state that affects later sales. Funnel: stages from awareness to purchase. Interactions: one input changes another's effect.

Time-varying graphs are our own research direction. The paper does not cover them.

Conclusions

An initial result toward
causal discovery in marketing

  1. 01Contribution inferenceLower channel CRPS than both regressions in all five conditions and at both horizons. About a quarter of the error of a PyMC-Marketing model with default priors.
  2. 02Constrained causal structure learningChannel-edge precision and recall near 0.9, with edge probabilities close to calibrated at the margin. The exact channel set in about half of the worlds.
  3. 03Reusable inferenceOne trained network serves every dataset, with no new fit, and the same answer in any units.

River shows that structure learning occurs in the tested direct-effect setting. It does not show that the current weights handle richer causal systems.

Three contributions, each with its limit.

River is a first tool, not the finished tool.
